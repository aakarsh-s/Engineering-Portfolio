Custom Robotic Arm

Arm Iterations

A progression from early CAD architecture to a physical prototype, with each revision improving stiffness, actuator packaging, mass distribution, and serviceability.

Back to Projects

Iteration 01 / Baseline Architecture

Initial shoulder and base layout

This revision established the main revolute joints, base turntable, belt-driven rotation concept, and high-level actuator placement.

  • Defined the base yaw axis and shoulder pitch geometry.
  • Explored pulley-driven torque transmission around the base.
  • Used broad structural plates to validate linkage spacing and joint clearances.

Iteration 02 / Structural Optimization

Trussed link and actuator integration

The arm moved toward a lighter link structure with triangular cutouts, improved actuator packaging, and a clearer load path from the end effector back into the shoulder joint.

  • Reduced link mass with truss-style geometry while preserving bending stiffness.
  • Improved motor packaging around the elbow and shoulder axes.
  • Added more accessible fastener locations for assembly and maintenance.

Iteration 03 / Physical Prototype

Printed assembly and electronics integration

The current prototype brings the CAD system into hardware, revealing real constraints around wiring, actuator mounting, torsional compliance, and center-of-mass management.

  • Validated printed part tolerances and mechanical fit-up.
  • Integrated servos, wiring, and power distribution around the moving joints.
  • Identified next improvements for cable routing, bearing support, and joint backlash reduction.
Load path Improved transfer of bending and torsional loads through the shoulder, elbow, and trussed link structure.
Actuation More deliberate actuator placement for torque delivery, packaging, and future closed-loop control.
Serviceability Better access to fasteners, wiring, and modular subassemblies for iteration and repair.
