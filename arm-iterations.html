Custom Robotic Arm

Arm Iterations

A focused progression of the custom robotic arm from manufacturable printed components to a full physical prototype, emphasizing stiffness, actuator packaging, transmission design, and serviceability.

Back to Projects

Phase 01 / Manufacturing Setup

Printed shoulder plate preparation

Early CAD moved into slicer preparation, where geometry had to be checked against print orientation, material settings, support needs, and hole tolerances before committing to physical parts.

  • Prepared a load-bearing shoulder side plate for additive manufacturing.
  • Checked printability of motor cutouts, mounting holes, and curved joint geometry.
  • Used slicing feedback to catch material/profile mismatches before fabrication.

Phase 02 / System Architecture

Baseline arm and turntable assembly

This full assembly established the main yaw base, shoulder pitch axis, belt-driven base rotation, arm links, actuator locations, and early end-effector placement.

  • Defined the major rotational axes and mechanical envelope.
  • Explored timing-belt transmission for high-ratio base motion.
  • Validated rough joint spacing before detailed part optimization.

Phase 03 / Linkage Development

Parallel linkage and gear-driven motion

The arm geometry was refined into a more controlled linkage architecture with paired members, gear coupling, and repeatable mounting points for precise joint motion.

  • Used paired links to improve alignment and reduce off-axis loading.
  • Added gear coupling to transfer motion between linked members.
  • Improved fastener access and symmetric load distribution.

Phase 04 / Actuator Stack-Up

Internal actuator packaging

A section view clarified the internal actuator stack-up, including shaft alignment, bearing support, radial packaging, and the interface between rotating and fixed structures.

  • Mapped the concentric shaft, bearing, and housing layout.
  • Reduced ambiguity around axial spacing and interference zones.
  • Designed around assembly order, retention, and service access.

Phase 05 / Integrated CAD Revision

Trussed links, base gearing, and gripper integration

The mature CAD assembly combined the base ring gear, shoulder structure, trussed arm segments, servo placement, and end effector into one coherent mechanism.

  • Reduced link mass with triangular cutouts while maintaining bending stiffness.
  • Integrated actuator mounts directly into structural members.
  • Improved the load path from gripper through elbow, shoulder, and base.

Phase 06 / End Effector

Geared gripper mechanism

The end effector became a dedicated geared subassembly rather than a simple claw, improving symmetry, closure control, and future compatibility with autonomous manipulation.

  • Used meshed gears to coordinate opposing finger motion.
  • Added pivot geometry and linkage spacing for stable gripping.
  • Separated the gripper module from the arm body for easier iteration.

Phase 07 / Physical Prototype

Printed assembly and electronics integration

The current prototype brings the CAD system into hardware, revealing real constraints around wiring, actuator mounting, torsional compliance, and center-of-mass management.

  • Validated printed part tolerances and mechanical fit-up.
  • Integrated servos, wiring, and power distribution around the moving joints.
  • Identified next improvements for cable routing, bearing support, and joint backlash reduction.
Load path Improved transfer of bending and torsional loads through the shoulder, elbow, and trussed link structure.
Actuation More deliberate actuator placement for torque delivery, packaging, and future closed-loop control.
Serviceability Better access to fasteners, wiring, and modular subassemblies for iteration and repair.
