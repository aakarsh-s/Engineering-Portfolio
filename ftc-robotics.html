Phase 01 / Preliminary Design

Initial chassis and shooter assembly

I developed the initial mechanical architecture in Onshape, establishing the chassis, shooter positioning, and preliminary ball-transfer geometry before integrating a complete intake. This prototype provided a physical reference for evaluating subsystem packaging and mechanical interfaces.

Phase 02 / Trajectory Calibration

Initial shooter kinematics testing

Conducted preliminary shooting tests to calibrate the kinematic calculations used to determine the required launch angle based on distance from the target. Physical testing helped establish the relationship between theoretical ball trajectories and actual shooter behavior.

Phase 03 / Controls Refinement

Shooter algorithm validation

Continued refining the shooter control code through repeated physical launch tests. Observed discrepancies between calculated and actual trajectories informed adjustments to the hood-angle calculations, improving shooting consistency across different distances from the target.

Phase 04 / Intake Iteration

Compliant rubber-band intake

The intermediate intake used flexible rubber bands to acquire and transport balls. Additional rubber wheels increased friction and partially improved acquisition, but inconsistent contact between the intake and ball surfaces continued to cause slippage. These limitations motivated a custom molded wheel design.

Phase 05 / Meet 3 Robot

Integrated intermediate competition robot

This iteration introduced lighter structural plates, a lowered intake ramp, more reliable internal ball transfer, and a functional adjustable shooter hood for scoring from different distances.

However, intake slippage and slow transfer to the elevated shooter limited scoring throughput to approximately one shot every three seconds, establishing the primary performance constraint for the final redesign.

Phase 06 / Structural Redesign

Compact and lightweight chassis

I redesigned the chassis in Onshape with a narrower footprint and pocketed structural side plates. The reduced dimensions improved maneuverability around field obstacles, while removing unnecessary material reduced drivetrain load and supported faster field traversal.

Phase 07 / Custom Component Manufacturing

High-friction silicone intake wheels

I designed custom intake wheel geometry and 3D-printed molds for casting silicone wheels with controlled dimensions and high-friction contact surfaces.

Two rows of custom wheels maintained continuous ball contact, while a third row of smaller Gecko wheels accelerated transfer into the internal mechanism. This addressed the slippage and inconsistent acquisition of earlier rubber-band designs.

Phase 08 / Turret and Shooter

Independent aiming and flywheel optimization

I designed a rotating turret that allowed target alignment independently of chassis orientation, minimizing repositioning between ball acquisition and scoring.

Weighted flywheels increased rotational inertia to reduce speed loss between launches. A 1:1 transmission ratio was retained as the preferred balance between torque and rotational speed.

Phase 09 / Field Testing

System-level validation

Field testing evaluated the interaction between the intake, active transfer, shooter, turret, sensing, and control software under realistic competition constraints. Testing informed refinements to subsystem timing, automated aiming, and scoring consistency.

Phase 10 / Final Competition Robot

Integrated competition platform

The final robot combined the compact chassis, custom molded silicone intake wheels, active transfer mechanism, rotating turret, adjustable shooter hood, and refined control software. I led the system-level mechanical design in Onshape, with peer assistance on select refinements.

~3 s → ~1 s Time between consecutive shots
~67% Reduction in time between shots
~3× Consecutive shooting rate

Final pre-competition testing demonstrated the combined improvements in ball acquisition, transfer speed, shooting consistency, and independent target alignment.
