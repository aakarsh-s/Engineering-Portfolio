ModalAI / Autonomous Drone Integration

From kit assembly to controlled flight testing.

A sophomore-year high school engineering project focused on taking a ModalAI-style autonomous drone kit from hardware assembly through software configuration, sensor bring-up, camera calibration, failure analysis, documentation, and flight validation.

Step 01 / System decomposition

Converted the kit into an engineering workflow.

We broke the project into hardware setup, software setup, and flight validation: frame, motors, cameras, chips, wiring, battery, RC setup, ADB, camera software, controller connection, QGroundControl, and final flight testing.

  • Mapped the build sequence before touching flight-critical settings.
  • Separated mechanical assembly, embedded configuration, and test operations.
  • Turned the workflow into an instruction manual for repeatability.

Step 02 / Hardware integration

Built the airframe and packaged the electronics.

The assembled drone combined a carbon frame, protected propeller layout, battery retention, motor assemblies, camera hardware, and onboard compute. This stage made the project more than a software demo: wiring, fasteners, mass distribution, and access to chips all affected whether the system could be tested safely.

  • Integrated motors, cameras, battery, chip stack, and wiring harnesses.
  • Checked mechanical clearance around propellers and protective ducts.
  • Prepared the system for RC/QGC bring-up and bench validation.

Step 03 / Bench debugging

Diagnosed issues at the component level.

Before stable flight, the project required close inspection of board connections and exposed wiring. The logs show the team documenting damaged parts, sending evidence to a mentor, and treating failures as engineering feedback rather than one-off mistakes.

  • Inspected GPS/ELRS-related wiring after a crash event.
  • Documented damaged conductors and connector-side issues.
  • Used the evidence to plan repair before attempting another flight.

Step 04 / Arming and thrust characterization

Moved from arming to lift-off diagnosis.

The drone eventually armed and spun its propellers, with the rear partially lifting. The system still lacked enough thrust for controlled takeoff, so the next engineering question became whether the limitation was software configuration, thrust mapping, motor output, or sensor state.

  • Verified arming behavior and propeller spin-up.
  • Observed partial lift rather than stable takeoff.
  • Identified thrust configuration and GPS connection as key follow-up items.

Step 05 / QGroundControl tuning and failure analysis

Increased thrust, then analyzed instability.

We updated the max RPM parameter in QGroundControl from 15000 to 32000 to generate adequate thrust. The drone lifted, but the response became unstable: it rose uncontrollably, did not descend properly during disarm, and crashed. The result was still useful: it proved the platform could generate lift and exposed a control and safety envelope problem.

  • Validated that insufficient thrust was not purely mechanical.
  • Observed unstable vertical response after the RPM increase.
  • Documented damaged wiring before continuing the test cycle.

Step 06 / Camera and VOXL bring-up

Connected the camera stack into the VOXL system.

The camera work required mapping the M0149 tracking camera and M0107 hires camera into the VOXL 2 Mini connector stack. Build logs show camera-chip connection work, verification through ADB shell, QGC, and VOXL portal, and continued debugging when the camera connection existed but the output was not yet visible.

  • Connected camera hardware into the VOXL chip stack.
  • Verified the connection through lower-level system tools.
  • Continued debugging the output path into VOXL portal/QGC.

Step 07 / Camera calibration

Calibrated the fisheye tracking camera.

The calibration workflow used the VOXL camera calibration tool with a 9x6 target and stored OpenCV tracking intrinsics. After multiple failed attempts due to finding a sufficiently flat target setup, the calibration succeeded and wrote data to the VOXL tracking intrinsics file.

  • Ran voxl-calibrate-camera tracking -f -s 9x6 -l 0.0165.
  • Captured a successful fisheye distortion model and intrinsic matrix.
  • Recorded a reprojection error of 0.491094 before saving calibration data.

Step 08 / Flight validation

Closed the loop with physical test evidence.

The flight video documents the project moving from bench assembly into real-world validation. Even when the system behavior exposed instability, the test cycle created useful data: thrust response, disarm behavior, damage modes, and the next set of changes needed for safer controlled flight.

  • Validated that software changes affected actual lift behavior.
  • Captured physical behavior rather than relying only on bench assumptions.
  • Converted the test outcome into repair, tuning, and documentation tasks.
